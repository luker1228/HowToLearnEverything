# Natural Expressions

Expressions learned from comparing original writing with corrected versions.

These show how to transform awkward phrasing into natural English.

---

| Instead of... | Say... | Why it's better |
|---------------|--------|-----------------|
| "has developed a very big improvement" | "has improved dramatically" | One strong verb + adverb replaces a vague noun phrase |
| "what can we get from AI" | "what benefits can we gain from AI" | More specific and formal |
| "dialog with someone with English" | "holding conversations in English" | Correct preposition, natural gerund phrase |
| "make my English better" | "improve my English" | Single precise verb is stronger than make + adjective |
| "make sure whether you're right or wrong quickly" | "verify correctness immediately" | Concise; one word replaces a clause |
| "in the next AI generation" | "in the AI era ahead" | More natural time reference |
| "really exciting" | "truly exciting" | Less colloquial, stronger emphasis |
| "for common people" | "for ordinary people" | "ordinary" is the standard collocation |
| "laughing like flu, even you do a good handwashing, you can't protect yourself against it." | "Laughter is like the flu: even if you wash your hands well, you still can't protect yourself from it." | Uses the noun "laughter", adds "the" before "flu", and uses natural conditional "even if" plus the common phrase "wash your hands". |
| "My logic is really mess." | "My logic is really a mess." | Adds the article "a" before the singular countable noun "mess", making the sentence grammatical and natural. |
