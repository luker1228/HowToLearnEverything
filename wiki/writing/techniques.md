# Writing Techniques

Principles and techniques for better English writing.

---

## From The Elements of Style

### 1. Omit Needless Words
Every word should earn its place. Cut filler like "really", "very", "in my opinion I think that".

### 2. Use Active Voice
- "AI provides" not "something is provided by AI"
- "I have resolved" not "a resolution has been made"

### 3. Use Parallel Structure
Items in a list must share the same grammatical form:
- "You write code, you run it, you see the result" (same pattern)
- "improve my English and explore other languages" (same verb form)

### 4. Use Definite, Specific, Concrete Language
Replace vague words with precise ones:
- "very big improvement" -> "dramatic improvement"
- "common people" -> "ordinary people"

### 5. Put the Emphatic Word at the End
Each sentence should end with its most important point:
- "previously closed to most people" (emphasizes impact)
- "instantly identify errors" (emphasizes speed)

### 6. Make the Paragraph the Unit of Composition
One topic per paragraph. Break long text into logical sections.

---

## Natural Transitions

Use transitions that feel organic, not mechanical:
- "While some worry... I believe..." (contrast)
- "For language learners especially" (example shift)
- "With these tools available" (linking paragraphs)

---

## Strong Conclusions

End paragraphs and essays with the most impactful point. Build toward it rather than trailing off.

---

## Humorous Metaphors

You can make your writing more vivid and memorable by using metaphors:
- "Laughter is like the flu" suggests that laughter can spread quickly from person to person.
- The sentence "even a good handwashing will not protect you against breaking into a fit of laughter" turns a health metaphor into a joke.

Use metaphors to:
- Compare an abstract idea (like joy or motivation) to something concrete (like weather or a virus)
- Add humor and personality to your writing
- Help the reader *feel* the idea, not just understand it logically.

---

## Sensory Details

Use the five senses to make descriptions vivid:
- **Sound**: "The sound of rain creates a soothing white noise."
- **Smell**: "The air smells clean and fresh after the rain" and the word "petrichor" for the smell after rain.
- **Sight**: "Everything looks greener and more vibrant."

Sensory details help the reader experience the scene, not just understand the facts.
