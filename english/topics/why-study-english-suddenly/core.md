## Canonical monologue (full story in order)

Use as the backbone. Writing = polish into paragraphs. Speaking = break into short answers under interview questions.

Why do I want to study English all of a sudden? It’s actually hard to answer.

Not exactly suddenly. For years I told myself I’d do it later. Later never came.

In primary school I did well in English. Gradually I spent less and less time on it — and more and more time on short videos. The result? I got a kind of empty happiness — and I lost a lot of my English ability. Thanks to my past efforts, I still have a pretty good foundation — but when I need to speak or write, I often can’t say what I mean. My output still lags far behind.

Why now? I want to change myself. I don’t want to spend every day on my phone chasing that empty happiness. I want to be someone who can listen to podcasts and watch longer videos without subtitles.

What’s more, as a programmer, logic is my default mode. Programming trains one kind of mind; language trains another — more feeling, more nuance. I want both.

So this time, “later” is not the plan. I want a long game that takes about 10,000 hours. Most of them should be speaking and writing. I don’t want English only as input; I want to produce it. I’m done chasing empty happiness. I want the real skill back.

Have you ever traded something that mattered for something that only felt easy?
