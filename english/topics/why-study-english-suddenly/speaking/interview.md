# Speaking practice — Interviewer continuous questions

> **Source:** always `../core.md` (same story).  
> **Skill:** oral answers under **follow-up pressure** (like an interviewer / curious friend).  
> **Rule:** each answer = **2–4 short sentences**. Do not dump the whole monologue in Q1.

---

## How to practice

1. Cover the **A** lines.  
2. Read **Q** out loud (or record yourself as interviewer).  
3. Answer in English for 15–40 seconds.  
4. Uncover **A (from core)** and compare — same facts, shorter shape.  
5. Full run: Q1→Q9 without stopping (mock interview, ~4–6 minutes).

---

## Continuous interview (Q chain)

Interviewer keeps digging. Your job: stay on **one core story**, add one new layer each turn.

---

### Q1 — Open

**Interviewer:** Why do you want to study English all of a sudden?

**A (from core):**  
Not exactly suddenly. For years I told myself I’d do it later. Later never came. It’s actually hard to answer in one line.

**Don’t:** give the full 90-second speech here. Leave room for follow-ups.

---

### Q2 — Why not earlier?

**Interviewer:** Then why not earlier? What changed?

**A (from core):**  
Earlier I always waited for “later.” Later never came. What changed is quieter — I got tired of postponing, and I want to change myself now.

---

### Q3 — The past

**Interviewer:** What was your English like before?

**A (from core):**  
In primary school I did well. Gradually I spent less and less time on English — and more and more time on short videos.

---

### Q4 — The cost (result)

**Interviewer:** So what did that cost you?

**A (from core):**  
The result? I got a kind of empty happiness — and I lost a lot of my English ability. That trade was bad.

---

### Q5 — Level now

**Interviewer:** How would you describe your English now?

**A (from core):**  
Thanks to my past efforts, I still have a pretty good foundation. But when I need to speak or write, I often can’t say what I mean. My output still lags far behind.

---

### Q6 — Career or personal?

**Interviewer:** Is this mainly for your career?

**A (from core):**  
Both, but the louder reason is personal. I want to change myself. I don’t want to spend every day on my phone chasing empty happiness. Career is in the mix; the real push is who I want to become.

---

### Q7 — Concrete goal

**Interviewer:** What does “good English” look like for you?

**A (from core):**  
I want to listen to podcasts and watch longer videos without subtitles. And I don’t want English only as input — I want to produce it: speak and write.

---

### Q8 — Why language as a programmer?

**Interviewer:** You’re a programmer. Why language? Why not only code?

**A (from core):**  
As a programmer, logic is my default mode. Programming trains one kind of mind; language trains another — more feeling, more nuance. I want both.

---

### Q9 — Plan + close

**Interviewer:** So what’s your plan? How serious is this?

**A (from core):**  
This time, “later” is not the plan. I want a long game that takes about 10,000 hours. Most of them should be speaking and writing. I’m done chasing empty happiness. I want the real skill back.

**Optional door (if chat, not formal interview):**  
Have you ever traded something that mattered for something that only felt easy?

## Bonus pressure questions (when Q1–Q9 feel easy)

Use the same core; invent no new biography.
