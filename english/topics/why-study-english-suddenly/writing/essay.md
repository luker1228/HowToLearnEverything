# Writing practice — Essay form

> **Source:** always `../core.md` (same story).  
> **Skill:** continuous writing — paragraphs, connectors, no interview Q.  
> **Length:** ~180–250 words (or 12–18 sentences).

---

## Task

Write (or rewrite) a short essay:

**Title:** Why I Want to Study English Now

**Do not** answer as Q&A. Write as one piece: opening → past → result → why now → plan → close.

---

## Structure map (from core)

| Paragraph | From core spine | Connectors you may use |
|-----------|-----------------|------------------------|
| 1 Opening | soft open · later never came | *Not exactly… / For years…* |
| 2 Past + result | school → short videos → empty happiness / lost English | *Gradually… / The result?* |
| 3 Gap now | past efforts · foundation · output lags | *Thanks to… / but when…* |
| 4 Why now | change myself · no empty happiness · no subtitles | *Why now? / I want to be someone who…* |
| 5 Deeper reason | programmer · two minds · I want both | *What’s more…* |
| 6 Plan + close | long game · 10,000 hours · output · real skill back | *So this time… / I’m done…* |

Optional last line (essay can keep or drop the door):  
*Have you ever traded something that mattered for something that only felt easy?*  
(In pure essay form, you may replace the door with a firm last sentence only.)

---

## Model essay (polished from core)

Why I want to study English “all of a sudden” is actually hard to answer. It is not exactly sudden. For years I told myself I would do it later. Later never came.

In primary school I did well in English. Gradually I spent less and less time on it — and more and more time on short videos. The result? I got a kind of empty happiness, and I lost a lot of my English ability. Thanks to my past efforts, I still have a pretty good foundation. But when I need to speak or write, I often cannot say what I mean. My output still lags far behind.

Why now? I want to change myself. I do not want to spend every day on my phone chasing that empty happiness. I want to be someone who can listen to podcasts and watch longer videos without subtitles. What’s more, as a programmer, logic is my default mode. Programming trains one kind of mind; language trains another — more feeling, more nuance. I want both.

So this time, “later” is not the plan. I want a long game that takes about 10,000 hours. Most of them should be speaking and writing. I do not want English only as input; I want to produce it. I am done chasing empty happiness. I want the real skill back.
