# word

## thoughtful

**Meaning:** Showing consideration for the needs and feelings of others (or yourself); mindful; deliberate; kind.

### Example Sentences

**About a person who is considerate:**
- "She's so thoughtful—she always remembers my birthday."
- "That was really thoughtful of you to help me with my homework."
- "My brother is thoughtful; he calls just to check on me."

**About actions/gifts:**
- "Thank you for the thoughtful gift."
- "It was a thoughtful gesture to bring soup when I was sick."

**About yourself (self-love context):**
- "I want to be more thoughtful about how I speak to myself."
- "I'm trying to be thoughtful with my time and energy."

**About thinking deeply:**
- "He gave a thoughtful answer after considering the question carefully."
- "She sat in thoughtful silence for a moment."

---

**Tip:** "Thoughtful" usually means **considerate/kind** (most common) or **deep in thought**. Context makes it clear!

## classic tired person answer

**Phrase people use when they actually mean the opposite.**

### Common Example:
> "Sure, no problem." = There **IS** a problem.

### What it means:
- You're tired / don't want to do something
- But you say "yes" to be polite
- You **lie** to avoid conflict
- Your body says no, your mouth says yes

### This is called:
- **People-pleasing**
- **Poor boundaries**
- **Not being honest with yourself**

### The Self-Love Lesson:
> **Be honest about your needs.**
> If you're tired, say "I'm tired."
> Don't sacrifice yourself to please others.

## That is such a real situation

**A warm, native-sounding way to say "I completely relate — that's so honest and human."**

### What it means:
- That is **so relatable** / that happens to me too
- You use it to **validate** someone — "I see you, I've been there"
- Perfect for small daily struggles everyone understands

### When to use it:
When a friend describes something honest, common, or universally human — especially a moment of weakness (saying "yes" when you're tired, pretending to understand, etc.).

### Example Sentences:

**Validating a relatable moment:**
- A: "I nodded in the meeting but I had no idea what they said."
  B: "Haha, that is such a real situation."
- A: "Our boss always wants us to finish impossible jobs on a tight deadline."
  B: "That is such a real situation."

### Grammar tip — such a vs. so:
- **such a** + adjective + noun → "such a **real situation**"
- **so** + adjective (no noun) → "that's **so real**"
- ❌ "that is **so** real situation" (`so` can't go directly before a noun)

### Pair it with (sounds even more native):
- "I feel that."
- "Story of my life."
- "I've been there."
- "So true."

## Loving yourself more means you stop leaving yourself last every single time.

**The podcast's core message — a beautiful, quote-style sentence about self-love.**

### What it means:
Real self-love isn't a feeling — it's the decision to **stop treating yourself as the lowest priority.**
("Leaving yourself last" = always putting everyone else's needs before your own.)

### Break it into 3 patterns:

**1. `leave yourself last` = put yourself at the bottom of the list**
- "She leaves herself last — she takes care of everyone except herself."
- 🔁 Opposite: **put yourself first** → "It's okay to put yourself first sometimes."

**2. `stop + -ing` = quit a habit**
- After **stop**, use the **-ing** form, never "to."
- ✅ "stop leaving yourself last" / "stop people-pleasing"
- ❌ "stop to leave yourself last" (= pause *in order to* leave — different meaning!)
- Spelling: **put → putting** (double the t: one syllable, consonant-vowel-consonant).

**3. `every single time` = emphasis 🔥**
- "single" adds emotional force — "every time, no exceptions."
- "He's late every single time."

### The reusable template ✨
> **"[Doing X] means you stop [doing Y]."**
- "Growing up means you stop blaming everyone else."
- "Self-respect means you stop saying yes when you mean no."
- "Being mature means you stop needing everyone to like you."

### Watch out: `anymore` ⚠️
- ❌ "you stop putting yourself last anymore" — redundant!
- `stop + -ing` already means the habit ended, so drop "anymore."
- ✅ "you stop putting yourself last."
- OR restructure with **don't ... anymore**: "you don't put yourself last anymore."

## irritated

**Feeling annoyed, on edge, slightly bothered — that prickly "everything is rubbing me the wrong way" feeling.**

### Two meanings (the key insight):
| | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical** | sore, red, itchy | "My eyes are irritated from the screen." |
| **Emotional** | annoyed, on edge | "I felt irritated by the noise." |

The podcast uses the **emotional** sense — when you're burnt out, you feel **irritated by everything**, even when nothing is wrong.

### ⚠️ `irritated` vs `irritating` (-ed = feeling · -ing = cause):
- ✅ "**I am irritated.**" = I feel annoyed. (describes **you**)
- ✅ "**That noise is irritating.**" = it annoys me. (describes **the thing**)
- ❌ "I am irritating." = …you annoy other people! 😅
- Same pattern as: **bored/boring, tired/tiring, interested/interesting.**

### Prepositions:
- irritated **by** + thing → "irritated by loud chewing"
- irritated **with** + person → "irritated with her brother"
- irritated **at** + situation → "irritated at the delay"

### Real-life examples 💬:
- "I feel so irritated when someone chews with their mouth open."
- "I felt so irritated — he replied 'ok' to my long message."
- "By 4pm everyone's tired and irritated."
- "Everything was irritating me — I didn't know why. I was just burnt out."

### The pattern to remember 🧠:
> Irritation = a small thing + a tired person → a bigger reaction than it deserves.
That gap — between the *tiny trigger* and the *big feeling* — is irritation.

### Pronunciation 🗣️:
**IRR-i-tay-tid** — stress on the **first** syllable, short "i" (like "it").
✅ "IRR-i-tay-tid" · ❌ "eye-ri-tay-tid"

## hand them the whole remote control for your mood

**A vivid metaphor: letting other people's actions decide how you feel.**

### What it means:
If someone is nice → you're happy. If they ignore you → you're sad. **They're holding the remote**; your mood changes with *their* buttons. 🎛️

Self-love = **you keep the remote.** No one else gets to change your channel.
(Playfully: *don't grant others write-access to your mood — you're the admin.* 🛡️)

### Breakdown:
- **hand [someone] [something]** = give/pass to someone (casual, vivid)
  - "Can you **hand me** that book?" (physical)
  - "She **handed** him **the power**." (metaphorical)
- **the remote control** = the TV device → *the power to control something*
- **for your mood** = the thing being controlled = your emotions
- **the whole** = emphasizes giving up ALL of it

### Same metaphor family 🔌 (native speakers love machine metaphors for feelings):
- **"push someone's buttons"** = know exactly how to trigger their emotions
  - "My brother really knows how to push my buttons."
- "he's a wreck" · "I'm running on empty" · "she's wired differently"
- "we're not on the same wavelength" · "he shut down"

### How to use it:
- "Stop **handing him the remote control for your mood**."
- "I'd been **giving everyone the remote** — no wonder I felt out of control."
- **"Self-love means you don't hand them the whole remote control for your mood."**
