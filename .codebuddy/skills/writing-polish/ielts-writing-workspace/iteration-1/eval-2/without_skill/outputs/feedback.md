# IELTS Essay Feedback: "Education Should Be Free for All Students"

## Original Essay

Education is very important for everyone. There is a question that should education be free for all students? I strongly agree with this statement because it can help poor students to access knowledge.

There are many reasons why free education is beneficial. First of all, it can reduce the financial burden on families. Many parents have to pay expensive tuition fees for their children. If education is free, these families can save a lot of money. Secondly, free education can promote social equality. Everyone should have the equal opportunity to receive good education regardless of their background.

However, some people argue that free education may cause some problems. For instance, the government needs to spend a lot of money to support free education. This might increase taxes. But I think this is worthwhile investment because educated population can contribute more to the society.

To sum up, I am of the opinion that education should be free for all students. It is essential for creating a fairer society and empowering individuals to reach their full potential.

## Analysis Based on The Elements of Style

### Rule 1: Choose a suitable design and hold to it

The essay follows a clear structure (introduction, body paragraphs, conclusion), which is appropriate for an IELTS essay. However, the introduction is weak and could be more direct.

**Issue**: The introduction contains awkward phrasing ("There is a question that should education be free...") that obscures the writer's position.

### Rule 2: Write in a way that draws the reader's attention

The essay presents a straightforward argument but lacks compelling hooks or vivid examples that would engage the reader. The arguments remain abstract without concrete illustrations.

### Rule 3: Put statements in positive form

Several sentences use negative constructions that could be reframed more powerfully:

| Original | Problem | Suggested Revision |
|----------|---------|-------------------|
| "I strongly agree with this statement because it can help **poor students**" | "Poor" is a negative label | "students from disadvantaged backgrounds" |
| "it can help...to access knowledge" | Passive phrasing | "enables students to acquire knowledge" |

### Rule 4: Use definite, specific, concrete language

The essay relies heavily on abstract concepts without specific support:

| Vague Language | More Concrete Alternative |
|----------------|---------------------------|
| "a lot of money" | "thousands of dollars annually" |
| "a fairer society" | "a society with greater socioeconomic mobility" |
| "reach their full potential" | "contribute meaningfully to the workforce" |

### Rule 5: Eliminate unnecessary words

Several phrases are wordy and could be trimmed:

| Original | Verbose | Concise |
|----------|---------|---------|
| "First of all" | X | "First," |
| "it can reduce the financial burden on families" | X | "it reduces families' financial burden" |
| "Many parents have to pay expensive tuition fees for their children" | X | "Many parents struggle to pay tuition" |
| "save a lot of money" | X | "save money" |
| "an the opinion that" | X | "believe that" |

### Rule 6: Avoid qualifiers that weaken

Words like "very," "quite," "somewhat," and "might" dilute the argument:

- "very important" - "essential"
- "a lot of money" - quantify if possible
- "This might increase taxes" - "This could increase taxes" (though even this hedge may be unnecessary in this context)

### Rule 7: Exploit the binary structure of sentences

The essay could benefit from more subordination and varied sentence structures, especially in the counterargument paragraph where the contrast between the objection and the rebuttal could be more sharply drawn.

**Current**: "However, some people argue that free education may cause some problems. For instance, the government needs to spend a lot of money to support free education. This might increase taxes. But I think this is worthwhile investment..."

**Improved**: "Critics argue that free education strains government budgets, potentially increasing taxes. However, this concern overlooks the long-term economic gains..."

### Rule 8: Avoid successive, coordinate structures

The phrase "First of all... Secondly..." works, but the essay would benefit from more sophisticated transitions and varied sentence openings.

### Rule 9: Keep related words together

The sentence "Everyone should have the equal opportunity to receive good education regardless of their background" keeps elements well together, but "the equal opportunity" is awkward—either "equal opportunity" or "the same opportunity."

### Rule 10: Make the paragraph the unit of composition

Each paragraph addresses a distinct point, which is good. However, paragraphs could be more fully developed with additional support (examples, statistics, or elaboration).

## Specific Grammar and Usage Notes

1. **Question formation error**: "There is a question that should education be free..." should be "There is a question: should education be free..."

2. **Article error**: "Everyone should have the equal opportunity" should be "Everyone should have equal opportunity" (no article before uncountable noun)

3. **Word form**: "worthwhile investment" is correct; "worthwhile" is the proper adjective form

4. **Parallel structure**: "contribute more to the society" - "society" does not need "the" when used in a general sense

## Sample Revised Essay

Education is essential for individual success and societal progress. I strongly believe that all students should have access to free education, as it empowers disadvantaged learners and promotes social mobility.

Free education offers two significant advantages. First, it alleviates the financial strain on families. Many parents struggle to afford tuition, and free education would allow them to redirect those funds toward other necessities such as housing, healthcare, and nutrition. Second, free education fosters social equality by ensuring that students from all backgrounds—whether wealthy or impoverished—can access quality instruction. This levels the playing field and enables talented individuals to pursue their ambitions regardless of their circumstances.

Critics contend that free education imposes a significant financial burden on governments, potentially necessitating higher taxes. While this concern is legitimate, it represents a worthwhile investment. An educated populace generates greater economic output, pays higher taxes over lifetimes, and requires less social support. The long-term returns far outweigh the initial costs.

In conclusion, I maintain that education should be free for all students. It is fundamental to building a more equitable society and enabling individuals to achieve their full potential.
