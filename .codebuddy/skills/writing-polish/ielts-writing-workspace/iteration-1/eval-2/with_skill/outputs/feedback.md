## Overall Suggestions

### Top 5 Priority Improvements

1. **Develop body paragraphs with specific evidence**
   - Add statistics, examples, or concrete details to support each point
   - Aim for 3-4 sentences per body paragraph with explanation + example

2. **Replace vague language with specific terms**
   - "good education" → "tertiary/university education"
   - "a lot of money" → "substantial savings" or specify amounts
   - "very important" → "essential" or "crucial"

3. **Fix grammatical errors**
   - Add missing articles: "a worthwhile investment"
   - Remove incorrect articles: "contribute to society" (no "the")
   - Use correct collocations: "access education" not "access knowledge"

4. **Vary sentence openings and connectors**
   - Replace "First of all" with topic sentence directly
   - Use "However" only once, in the counter-argument paragraph
   - Let sentences flow logically without explicit transitions where possible

5. **Strengthen the counter-argument paragraph**
   - Acknowledge the objection more fully before refuting
   - Use specific reasoning: "Although free education requires significant government spending, the long-term economic benefits outweigh the costs"

## Sample Essay

**Topic:** Education should be free for all students. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

**Sample Response (Band 7+):**

I strongly agree that education should be free for all students, as this policy creates a more equitable society while generating substantial long-term economic benefits.

The primary advantage of free education is its potential to eliminate financial barriers for disadvantaged students. When tuition fees exist, talented individuals from low-income families often cannot afford higher education, regardless of their academic abilities. By removing these fees, governments enable all citizens to pursue their education based purely on merit, not wealth. Consequently, this levels the playing field and promotes genuine social mobility.

Furthermore, investing in free education yields significant economic returns for society as a whole. Educated citizens tend to secure higher-paying jobs, which increases their tax contributions over their lifetimes. They are also more likely to innovate, start businesses, and contribute to economic growth. While some argue that free education imposes a heavy financial burden on governments, this investment ultimately pays for itself through a more skilled and productive workforce.

In conclusion, I maintain that education should be free for all students. This policy not only ensures equal access to learning opportunities but also strengthens the economy by developing a well-educated population capable of driving future progress.
